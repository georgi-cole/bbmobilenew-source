# Player character roster and coverage plan

Status: planning proposal for the character-pack direction. This document does not approve existing candidates or authorize their release. Retain the six new identities already started; plan six more, for **12 player identities at launch**. Existing AI cast members, including Nova, remain separate. No new art is generated by this planning task.

The launch target is 12 complete character packs, each containing six primary scene images and a derived store thumbnail. The earlier 24-identity target is a possible expansion pool, not the launch commitment. Twelve is a bounded coverage decision, not a statistically optimal sample or a claim that every phenotype can be represented.

## What players choose

Players choose the complete character that best approximates them or the person they want to play. They can preview all scene versions before selecting or buying a pack. Hair, eyes, skin, face, body and apparent age belong to the authored character; these are not independent sliders in this release. Portrait accessories add compatible layers without promising arbitrary changes to the body or hairstyle.

Character choice does not infer the player's ethnicity, orientation, gender, age or medical history. Player name and pronouns are profile choices, independent of the selected illustration and optional character biography. Existing gameplay relationship or gender rules must not be silently changed by a cosmetic selection; any compatibility mapping belongs in the integration contract and its tests.

## Representation rules

- Use distinct original adult faces. Existing cast images may guide written art direction and visual comparison, but may not be supplied as identity references or copied into the new roster.
- Represent East, South and Southeast Asian backgrounds through distinct proposed fictional identities. An appearance label does not establish a nationality or subregion: a specific optional biography must be deliberately written and reviewed.
- Latine and Afro-Latine backgrounds are proposed fictional biographies, not facts guessed from skin, hair or facial structure. The art directions below describe visible characteristics separately.
- Queer and nonbinary representation comes from optional fictional biographies and self-description. Clothing, makeup, haircut and body shape do not establish orientation or gender. An androgynous presentation is available without locking pronouns to that presentation.
- Include a person with albinism as an ordinary complete character. Use natural pale skin and hair, light brows/lashes and blue-gray eyes for this particular fictional person. Avoid red-eye shorthand, glow or a fantasy treatment. This one character is not a template for every person with albinism; albinism is not a gameplay modifier.
- All authored characters are visibly adult. Ten of the proposed 12 appear 18–29, while two cover older adults. Marketing may target 16–29 without requiring a minor character roster.
- Basic representation remains available free. Skin tones, ethnicity, albinism, orientation and gender identity are never purchase categories, rarity labels, stat bonuses or premium traits.

## Complete-pack production checklist

Each identity needs these six accepted primary images. The architecture document owns final filenames, dimensions, path conventions and scene resolution behavior.

| Logical image           | Continuity requirement                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Casual full figure      | Front-facing complete figure, transparent background, safe hands/hair/feet margins; serves as the store preview                                                             |
| Main gameplay avatar    | Large readable face with established black/gold presentation; same identity and casual outfit cues                                                                          |
| Alternative grey avatar | Same identity, with composition matched to the existing lux-grey presentation; use a shared clean portrait foreground where this preserves the established visual treatment |
| Formal full figure      | The same face, body, apparent age and defining hair; a deliberately formal authored outfit                                                                                  |
| Sad avatar              | Expression edit of the main avatar that preserves pose, framing, identity and accessory fit; respectful sadness rather than exaggerated distress                            |
| Paparazzi image         | Recognizable same person in the established After the Eye scene treatment; a separate authored scene image when framing or pose demands it                                  |

Generate store/card thumbnails from accepted source art; they are derivatives, not an extra identity design. Twelve complete packs imply **72 primary images plus at least 12 derived thumbnails**. Existing premium streetwear samples for 01 and 02 do not count as formal attire without an explicit formal-art review. They may be retained as future fashion-edition candidates.

Before creating each set, freeze an identity sheet containing visible traits, face landmarks, body proportions, age target, hair silhouette and approved color reference. Use only that new identity's accepted sources for continuity edits. Record source, prompt, generation lineage and final asset hashes. A pack remains unavailable in the catalog until all mandatory scene images pass visual and technical review.

The current disk inventory in `look-pilot/masters/` includes main portraits only for 01 and 02. Its README still describes the earlier two-identity proof; body files for 03–06 are candidates and must not be interpreted as completed or accepted packs.

## Portrait accessory implications

- Start with frames and a small glasses proof. Glasses require per-character placement and overlap review in main, grey and sad portraits. A clothing image or full-body photo must not be silently substituted for a portrait accessory preview.
- Identity 02 has glasses painted into its current sources and is initially eyewear-incompatible. Exclude it from CS-01 and use 01 plus an eligible second source such as 03. Any later glasses-free replacement needs its own explicitly scoped art work and acceptance; no incidental source replacement or second pair drawn over the first.
- Keep existing essential or signature traits in the free character pack. Changing presentation must not require buying a person's identity or removing a representation trait.
- Wigs, hair-color controls and body clothing swaps require prepared underlying art and are deferred from this roster plan. A separate fashion edition preserves identity across its supported images and has its own compatibility/version record.
- Optional bio labels should not become store filters for race, orientation, albinism or gender. Useful browsing filters describe visible style: hair length/color, silhouette and outfit style, with an accessible text description for every character.

4. **CS-15B, after A is accepted:** complete packs 05–08, including the albinism/South Asian and androgynous Latine directions. Do not begin C before B's acceptance gate.
5. **CS-15C, after B is accepted:** complete packs 09–12, then review all 12 identities, the eight-free/four-unlock allocation and all 72 primary images together. Catalog availability remains conditional on acceptance; do not ship partial packs.

Freeze accepted source and manifest revisions as each ticket requires. The menu, profile selector, store ownership, equip logic, currency handling and regression checks are defined by the implementation backlog, not this art document.

Visual acceptance includes same-person consistency across all six scenes, distinctness from the existing cast and each other, plausible anatomy, hair/skin/eye consistency, clean cutout edges, framing in actual scene bounds, and readability at shipped avatar sizes. Check eyes in the larger preview; do not promise that green versus hazel remains distinguishable at 32 pixels. Document user acceptance separately from automated size/format checks.

## Known gaps and expansion trigger

Twelve identities cannot cover all faces or backgrounds. Current gaps include short/long red hair, older feminine and androgynous faces, additional Asian and Latine backgrounds, bald characters across presentations, more facial-hair styles, larger body and height variation, visible disability and mobility-aid representation, and many different facial structures within each visible trait group. These are candidate expansions, not evidence that a certain group is absent from the player base.

After release, collect voluntary feedback such as “I could not find a character close enough to me,” alongside selection, preview and purchase metrics. Record requested visual traits in aggregate; do not infer protected identities or medical status from a player's chosen art. A character's purchase count reflects access, price and presentation as well as appeal. Reassess gaps with player feedback and tester review before expanding toward 18 or 24 complete packs. New core representation should continue to reach the free selection as the roster grows.
