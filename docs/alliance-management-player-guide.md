# Alliance management: player guide

This guide covers personal pacts and groups in Reality Mode. It explains where
to find the controls, what each approval means, and what changes when somebody
joins or leaves.

## The short version

- A **personal pact** is a private one-to-one commitment. Each pair can have one
  current pact, and you may have any number of separate pacts.
- A **group** starts with at least three people, can grow to six, and stays a
  group if it later has only two members. Each player can belong to at most two
  groups at once.
- Joining a group formally aligns you with every current member. It does not
  create a separate personal pact with each person.
- Group membership influences AI strategy, but it does not force everybody to
  vote together or prevent betrayal.
- Pact, group, and admission requests appear in **Incoming requests**. A group
  invitation is sent to its candidate only after the current members approve.
- Sending a personal pact proposal costs ⚡1. Founding a group costs ⚡2, plus
  ⚡1 for each selected founder above the minimum roster. Proposing an admission
  or renewing an older pact costs ⚡1. Votes, accepting or declining, leaving a
  group, removing a member, and dissolving it are free.

## Where to manage alliances

1. Open **Social** from the game controls.
2. In **Your move**, choose the **Alliances** category. The action cards below
   it are the alliance controls available for your selected person or group.
3. Choose an action card. The card becomes your prepared move; it does not run
   yet.
4. Review the people selected in **Choose a hubmate**, then press **Execute** at
   the bottom of Social.
5. For requests that need your later response, open **Incoming requests** from
   the game controls. Its badge can include alliance votes and invitations as
   well as other incoming interactions.

To review current groups, officers, status, cohesion, and information you have
learned about other groups, open **My Game → Hub**.

## Propose a personal pact

1. In Social, select the person you want a pact with.
2. Choose **Alliances**.
3. Select **Propose personal pact** (or **Renew pact** for an older unresolved
   pact).
4. Press **Execute**.
5. The other person must agree. If they are an AI, the game records their
   decision; if they are human, they answer in **Incoming requests**.

When both people agree, the pact becomes active. It remains independent from
any groups either person belongs to. Either partner can end it by selecting the
other person, choosing **Alliances → End pact**, and pressing **Execute**.

## Found a group

1. Open Social and choose **Alliances**.
2. Select **Found an alliance**. This switches the roster to group selection.
3. Tap at least two other active hubmates. With you, that makes at least three
   founders. The roster hint shows how many people are selected.
4. Press **Execute** to send the proposed roster for consent.
5. Every named founder must agree. Your own initiating agreement is recorded
   when you submit the proposal.

The founding cost is paid when you submit and is not refunded if someone later
declines or the request expires. When everyone agrees, the game confirms the
founders and the group's name, for example: “You, Kian, and Jax made it official:
The Shield is born.” The person who starts this Social action becomes its
leader. The group can hold up to six people. Founding a separate group leaves
your existing personal pacts in place.

## Invite someone into one of your groups

1. Select the person you want to invite in Social.
2. Choose **Alliances**.
3. If you belong to more than one group, choose the **Invite [name]** card whose
   description names the group you mean.
4. Press **Execute** to start that group's admission vote.
5. The current members vote. The request passes only if more than half of the
   full current roster votes yes and at least one leader or co-leader votes yes.
   Your own vote counts as yes when you start the vote.
6. If the group approves, the candidate receives a separate invitation in
   **Incoming requests**. They must choose **Accept invitation** before their
   membership begins.

An accepted candidate joins as a regular member. They become formally aligned
with all current group members, but they do not automatically receive a
co-leader role or separate personal pacts. A candidate can belong to no more
than two groups, and the group must have a free place.

### How many yes votes are needed?

The proposer is already counted as one yes. The group needs a strict majority
of its roster, plus at least one officer's yes vote.

| Current members | Minimum yes votes |
| --------------: | ----------------: |
|               3 |                 2 |
|               4 |                 3 |
|               5 |                 3 |
|               6 |                 4 |

A no vote does not automatically defeat a proposal if enough other members can
still meet both requirements. A candidate's own acceptance comes after this
vote; it is not included in the member tally.

## Respond to an invitation or vote

1. Watch the badge on **Incoming requests** in the game controls.
2. Open it and find **Alliance proposals and votes** in **Incoming
   Interactions**.
3. Read the request title and roster before responding.
4. Choose the action that matches your role:
   - **Approve vote / Vote no** when your group is deciding whether to invite a
     candidate.
   - **Accept invitation / Decline invitation** when the group has approved
     your own admission.
   - **Agree to group / Decline proposal** for a group-founding proposal.
   - **Accept pact / Decline pact** for a one-to-one pact.
   - **Accept role / Decline role** for a co-leader offer, or **Accept
     leadership / Decline transfer** for a leadership offer.
   - **Approve removal / Decline suggestion** when an officer is considering a
     member-removal suggestion.

An AI-led group proposal can be created by expanding an existing personal pact.
If every founder agrees, that original pact may be replaced by the new group
(except for a pact marked as an endgame commitment). A group you start yourself
with **Found an alliance** is separate and leaves your pacts in place. The
current incoming card does not identify a pact that an AI proposal may replace;
see the product-owner notes for this interface gap.

You do not need to open Hub to discover an invitation. Requests use the shared
game clock and can expire if the game advances past their response window. The
current inbox does not show an exact alliance-request countdown, so answer
before moving through several game phases.

## Leave, remove, or dissolve

- **Leave a group:** select any current member, choose **Alliances → Leave
  [group name]**, then press **Execute**. You leave that group only; your other
  groups and pacts remain.
- **Leave a personal pact:** select its other partner, choose **End pact**, then
  press **Execute**. The other partner can also end it.
- **Remove a regular member:** a leader or co-leader selects that member and
  chooses **Remove [name]**. A co-leader cannot remove the leader or another
  officer; the leader can remove a co-leader.
- **Suggest a removal:** a regular member selects the person and chooses
  **Suggest removing [name]**. The officers decide whether to approve it.
- **Dissolve a group:** only the leader can choose **Dissolve [group name]**.
  This ends the group for everybody. Independent pacts remain.
- **Change roles:** only the leader can offer co-leadership or transfer
  leadership. The nominee must accept. The leader can also clear the co-leader
  role.
- **Rename a group:** only its leader can rename it in **My Game → Hub**.

If a group falls below two members, it ends. A former group with two members
remains a group; it does not silently become a personal pact.

## What membership changes in play

Group members become formal allies with each other. Their alliance can influence
AI nomination and voting strategy, and group discussions can establish shared
targets and fallback plans. Stronger commitment and cohesion make the strategic
protection more influential.

Forming a pact or group also strengthens the pairwise relationships among its
members. That boost is larger when people have known each other longer, have a
stronger relationship, and share more successful or positive history. Negative
history and unresolved grievances soften it. Admitting someone strengthens
their links to the group's current members. This is a relationship nudge, not a
promise of loyalty, protection, or matching votes.

Membership is not a shield. AI players can break ranks, follow a conflicting
target plan, or betray an ally. The game does not synchronize human or AI votes.
A betrayal by itself does not automatically expel the betrayer or cancel every
other commitment. The betrayer remains connected through any group they still
belong to; other pacts and groups are separate. Leaving or being removed ends
only that group's membership connection. Trust, promises, information, and
personal history are tracked separately and are not erased when someone leaves.

## Reading the Hub

- **New group / New pact** describes a newly formed commitment. It is not a vote
  result.
- **Active**, **Fractured**, and **Dormant** describe the commitment's current
  strategic health. They do not determine who is formally a member.
- **Cohesion** is a model of how consistently members are committed and aligned
  on plans. “50% cohesion” does not mean half the group voted yes, a 50% chance
  of admission, or that half the members are loyal.
- **Secrecy** describes how exposed the group is. A highly secret group can
  still leak or become known.
- **Leader**, **Co-leader**, and member standing are different things. Only the
  leader and co-leader can start direct member removal; social status does not
  grant officer authority.
- Entries about groups you are not in reflect only what you have learned.
  Missing members or a suspected pact may be hidden because your player has not
  discovered them.

## Common situations

**I have a pact with A and B and want to join C.** Keep your one-to-one pacts
and ask to join C's group, or form a new group with the people you choose. You
can be in up to two groups; a new group needs approval from every founder.

**I belong to two groups. How do I choose where D joins?** Select D and start
the admission vote from the card whose description names the intended group.
The request is tied to that group; it is not a vote across both groups.

**D joined my group. Are D and A, B, and C allies now?** Yes, as members of that
group. No extra pairwise pact requests are created.

**Will I be told if an AI invites me?** The AI proposal becomes an actionable
alliance item in **Incoming requests** after the group's member vote passes.
The dock badge counts it. It is not an invitation you need to search for in
Hub.

**If A betrays B, does A lose C and D too?** Not automatically. A keeps the
group membership until A leaves, an authorized officer removes A, or the group
ends. Any separate pact with C or D remains independent.

**Can I make an AI group without asking the human player?** AI founders still
need every named founder's consent. If you are one of the proposed founders,
you get a request to accept or decline.

**Can I manage alliances in Survival or the finale?** Alliance management is
disabled in Survival. Terminal and season-ending phases close pending decisions.
