# Final-day presentation direction

## Approved visual language

The Classic human Final Power decision is the approved visual reference. Keep the modular architecture, desk, Eye, monitor housing, cast and atmosphere independently controllable. Text belongs on the live monitor, with the main game's Montserrat styling, a smoked glass surface and restrained illuminated edges.

Normal speeches remain complete on one screen. Size and spacing adapt to the monitor; exceptional custom copy stays accessible without shrinking below the readable minimum. Names belong within the broadcast. The Eye stays above the holder, clear of the monitor and crown throughout the camera movement.

A continuous slow dolly connects the beats, with coordinated depth movement across architecture, cast and foreground. Reframes and lighting follow the current speaker. After the verdict, clear the other figures and desk, center the departing finalist under red light and desaturate the room. Retain the same music element through the real exit and fade it after the departure handoff.

Automatic entry and the existing shared Skip are part of the approved direction. Human choices remain explicit. Presentation controls never choose an outcome or change game rules.

Reuse the approved set for an AI Final Power holder. Resolve and retain the outcome and reason through the existing season-based decision function. The presentation reveals that result exactly once and hands off to the existing exit and Final Two continuation.

The human holder and all three AI-holder viewing scenarios are approved. Retain the real royal/front/exit poses, fitted crown anchors, outcome concealment before the verdict, compact phone layouts, guarded Skip, native fallback, foreground suspension and continuous soundtrack as the shared baseline.

## Approved: Vox Final Three audience verdict

Reuse the modular set with cooler violet light, a crownless immunity holder in their normal outfit, and the glass monitor as the source of audience information. The film covers the human being immune, a nominee who stays, a nominee who leaves, and a spectator outside the final cast.

1. Identify the final immunity holder independently from the nominee ballot. The monitor shows **IMMUNE**, without assigning the protected player a vote share.
2. Present both nominees' complete appeals. A human nominee chooses **The fight**, **The people** or **The game** inside the ceremony. The existing appeal action changes real audience approval before the vote is resolved. An unanswered choice never expires, and Skip cannot answer it.
3. Close the vote and request the existing game calculation once. Keep the same scene mounted while the store commits its result.
4. Show **VOTE TO ELIMINATE** with both nominee names and one-decimal percentages. Both numbers start at 0.0% and rise on one synchronized eased clock to the already committed totals. A restrained glass sweep and soft settling glow finish the reveal; there are no bars. Hold the exact values for three seconds before the verdict.
5. Give the result time to read, then isolate the committed departing player in red and continue into the existing exit with the same music element. After the doors close and the zoom finishes, the original purple/gold transition frame carries the rotating Eye with **THE BIG EYE** below. The final-day exit holds that branded frame before the existing Vox callback finalizes third place.

The normal Vox Final Three resolver already commits an evictee even when the displayed percentages round to 50.0%–50.0%. The presentation follows that stored outcome. It does not infer a winner from rounded values or invent an approval tiebreak. The renderer can display a separately labelled approval frame only when a caller explicitly supplies a genuine approval-tiebreak result; the ordinary Final Three adapter does not supply one.

The current-scene identity includes the game, season, week, immunity holder and nominees. Superseded callbacks cannot submit an appeal, resolve a vote, consume a broadcast or dismiss a replacement game's result. Resolved-result reloads retain native progression instead of replaying appeals. Standard, reduced-motion, disabled, short-viewport, unsupported-art and failed-preparation paths also retain the native flow.

Review all four roles through recordings from the real scene, game appeal/vote calculation and exit renderer. Check compact appeal controls, whole speeches, long names beside percentages, the human in either nominee position, delayed resolution, ties, required-choice Skip behavior and music through departure.

The three AI-only rounds now use an automatic garden broadcast with the approved modular Eye, glass monitor and full-size cast. Moving beams, mist, reflections and continuous camera depth connect each round's introduction, challenge coverage, result lock and solo winner reveal. The shared Skip control advances the existing result; there is no Begin or Pause button.

| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |

| Part 3 · The Final Power Battle | Down Memory Lane, with a question drawn from the real season when available | Finalist outside the duel, or eliminated viewer       |

Parts 1 and 2 reveal who advances to Part 3. The last round distinguishes Classic's final power from Vox Populi's final immunity. The real player retains the existing interactive challenge whenever they are a competitor.

AI-only rounds record the participant IDs, winner and seed; they do not record points or a question-by-question match. Monitor graphics therefore illustrate the actual challenge mechanics without inventing scores, leaders, answers or lost lives. Existing deterministic selectors supply the winner that the reducer will commit. The broadcast reveals it once, then releases its spectator gate before the existing phase advance. Only newly emitted result cards already represented by the film are consumed.

The normal round films last 25.6 seconds for the Circuit and 23.4 seconds for Down Memory Lane, including a readable winner hold. Backgrounding or losing presentation ownership suspends their active clocks and visual effects. The existing Final Three music remains continuous; brief, abortable accents mark the opening, result lock and reveal. Standard, reduced-motion, short-viewport, unsupported-art and preparation-failure routes offer a static result and shared Continue control. Run, phase, seed and cast checks prevent an old callback from advancing a replacement game; saved internal Classic Part 1 states resume correctly.

The set reuses existing artwork and adds no bitmap assets or dependencies. Review the eliminated viewer, the qualified Part 1 winner, and both final-duel rule sets on compact and tall phones, including Skip, foreground suspension and the actual committed result.

The later Vox Final Two audience ballot uses **VOTE TO WIN** and celebratory emphasis for the winner. Keep those semantics distinct from the Final Three elimination ballot.
